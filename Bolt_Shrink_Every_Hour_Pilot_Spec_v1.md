# WHAT IF YOU SHRUNK BY HALF EVERY HOUR?
## Bolt High-Retention Pilot Production Spec
**Pilot Spec v1.0 — Retention-engineered production package**
> **Operating rule:** One transformation. A complete new consequence every 3–5 seconds. A new scale world every 10–15 seconds. The story remains understandable when muted.
## Executive decision
**GO TO ANIMATIC.** The concept scores 30/30 and is unusually well suited to the retention framework because a single predictable rule creates many unpredictable daily-life consequences.

## 2. Strategic brief
- **Working title:** What If You Shrunk by Half Every Hour?
- **Core question:** How quickly would an ordinary home become impossible—and what would become the final threat?
- **One-sentence answer:** Shrinking makes you lighter, proportionally stronger, and safer from falls, but absolute strength collapses and surface forces dominate until water and airflow become serious hazards.
- **Governing rule:** Bolt’s height halves at the top of every hour; density and geometric proportions remain constant.
- **Bolt’s objective:** Cross one continuous apartment and press a red emergency reset switch before Hour 8.
- **Primary retention mechanism:** Countdown + changing scale worlds + pursuit + advantage-to-danger reversal.
- **Hidden consequence 1:** Falling becomes less dangerous instead of more dangerous.
- **Hidden consequence 2:** A floor vent becomes a wind tunnel capable of launching Bolt.
- **Hidden consequence 3:** A water droplet becomes a sticky, enclosing trap.
- **Final reversal:** Bolt is extremely strong relative to his own weight but too weak in absolute terms to press a human-sized switch.
- **Target runtime:** 58–64 seconds; retain only scenes that deliver a distinct consequence.
- **Pilot goal:** Test whether consequence density and changing story worlds outperform the ocean-depth list structure.

## 3. Scientific assumptions
- **Geometric scaling:** If every linear dimension scales by L, surface area scales with L² and volume/mass with L³.
- **Strength-to-weight:** Structural/muscle cross-section falls with L² while weight falls with L³; relative strength therefore improves as size falls.
- **Absolute strength:** Despite higher relative strength, absolute force output still falls rapidly; full-size switches and heavy objects become impossible.
- **Falling:** Terminal speed depends on mass relative to frontal area; under geometric similarity it declines approximately with the square root of linear size.
- **Surface forces:** At millimeter scale, capillary and surface-tension forces become large relative to body weight; water can adhere, pin, or envelop.
- **Biology boundary:** A real human could not remain biologically functional through these scales. The pilot isolates external scaling physics and uses Bolt as a visual proxy.

## 6. Final transcript

Every hour, you shrink to half your height. By Hour Eight, a fall barely matters—but one water drop can trap you. Bolt has eight hours to reach the reset switch.

Hour One: child-sized. The door handle is already overhead.

Hour Two: his phone weighs more than he does. But weight shrinks faster than strength, so he can shove it across a floor vent like a bridge.

Hour Three: he falls from the counter—and walks away. For a moment, small is useful. He steals an RC car.

Then the house changes sides. At four inches, carpet becomes a forest. At two inches, the cat attacks. At one inch, a house spider is nearly his size—and a floor vent can launch him across the room.

Hour Eight: Bolt is six millimeters tall and weighs only a few milligrams. He reaches the switch—but proportional strength is not absolute strength. He cannot press it.

Then one drop rolls toward him. At this scale, surface tension acts like a sticky wall.

Shrinking makes falls safer and you stronger for your size—but ordinary water becomes a cage.

## 9. Automatic rejection criteria
- Opening begins with a title card or calm setup.
- Any scale change is communicated only by a label or size meter.
- Bolt stands beside a familiar object without attempting to use it.
- Two adjacent scenes use the same consequence category or framing.
- The cat or spider becomes fantasy-horror rather than a plausible household threat.
- The switch activates despite the intended absolute-strength failure.
