## 2. CENTRAL ARGUMENT

Ormund did not take Tumbleton because he needed its walls. He took a town that had declared for Rhaenyra because its population changes the price of every response available to her.

Rhaenyra possesses greater firepower. But dragonfire cannot distinguish a Hightower soldier from a loyal citizen standing beside him. If she burns the occupiers, she burns people who raised her banner. If she does nothing, Ormund demonstrates that her protection is only symbolic. If she sends an army, he gains time and forces her to fight on terms that do not exploit her greatest advantage.

The episode therefore presents power as four connected resources:

1. **Territory** — who physically occupies the ground.
2. **Firepower** — who can destroy the enemy.
3. **Legitimacy** — whom the population accepts as lawful.
4. **Human cost** — what using power will do to the people a ruler claims to protect.

Rhaenyra has the first three in different degrees, but the fourth determines what she is allowed to do. Ormund's real weapon is not Tumbleton. It is Rhaenyra's need to remain a queen after the battle is over.

### Viewer promise

By the end, the viewer will understand:

- why Tumbleton matters even without exceptional fortifications;
- why dragons are poorly suited to clearing an occupied friendly town;
- why attacking, waiting, and marching an army all carry different losses;
- why Ormund's treatment of the residents is part of the military plan;
- what Rhaenyra's least-bad response actually is.

### Final verdict

Rhaenyra cannot burn Tumbleton because destroying Ormund's position would also destroy the political meaning of her own claim. Her best answer is containment, intelligence, evacuation, and a conventional encirclement—not indiscriminate dragonfire.
