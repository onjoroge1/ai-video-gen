# The Dinosaur Named After a Mistake — rebuilt Short brief

**Status:** script and beat table for approval. No clip has been bought. The only paid step so far
is timing the narration with the intended voice (onyx), which the brief asked for. Render spec:
`spec/oviraptor_short.json`; one command per stage in `scripts/keyframe_short.py` after sign-off.

**One question under the whole video:** *What mistake gave this dinosaur its name?*

---

## 1. Five hooks

1. **A skeleton, lying on a nest of eggs. Guilty, right?**
   Crime-scene framing. The first frame *is* the evidence, and the question invites the viewer to
   convict, which is exactly the mistake the story corrects.
2. **For seventy years this dinosaur was a thief. Then someone found a baby in the egg.**
   Time-span plus the clue. Strong, but it gives away the embryo in the first breath.
3. **The man who named the egg thief warned the name was probably wrong. Nobody could prove it for seventy years.**
   The naming irony up front. Good payoff material, weaker as an opener because it needs a name
   the viewer has not met.
4. **Oviraptor means egg thief. The eggs it was caught with were its own.**
   Contradiction in one breath. Clean, but it resolves the mystery before the evidence is shown.
5. **This dinosaur got a criminal record in 1924. The evidence was its own children.**
   Playful, concrete. "Criminal record" over-personifies a taxonomy decision.

**Selected: hook 1.** It works for someone who knows nothing about the animal, because the
opening frame shows the whole case (bones on eggs) and the question asks the viewer to reach the
same wrong verdict the scientists did. Hooks 2 and 4 spend the reveal; hook 1 keeps it for beat 4
while still paying off within eight seconds with the name.

## 2. Narration

**114 words · 40.2 s spoken (onyx, measured) · ~43.8 s with 0.45 s picture pads**

> A skeleton, lying on a nest of eggs. Guilty, right?
>
> In 1923, fossil hunters in Mongolia found exactly that, and named the dinosaur Oviraptor. Egg thief.
>
> The eggs, they assumed, belonged to a neighbour. A frilled dinosaur that nested nearby.
>
> Then, in 1994, the same kind of egg turned up with a baby inside. Not the neighbour's. An oviraptor's.
>
> The eggs were never stolen. They were its own.
>
> A year later, a close cousin turned up crouched over a clutch, arms spread like a brooding bird.
>
> We had mistaken a nursery for a crime scene.
>
> The man who named it had warned the name might mislead. Seventy years later, it still says egg thief.

Four words over the 110 ceiling, at natural pace, inside the 35–45 s window. The words that
could go are in beat 3 ("that nested nearby"), see section 5.

## 4. Source map and unresolved uncertainty

| Claim in narration | What is directly preserved | What is inferred | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Skeleton found on a nest of eggs, Mongolia, 1923; named Oviraptor ("egg seizer/thief") in 1924, species name "fond of ceratopsian eggs" | Holotype AMNH 6517 with eggs, Djadokhta Formation, Bayn Dzak (Flaming Cliffs), Third Central Asiatic Expedition | — | Osborn 1924, *AMNH Novitates* 144; [Wikipedia summary with citations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oviraptor) |
| The eggs were assumed to be Protoceratops's | Osborn's 1924 interpretation | The assumption itself | Osborn 1924 |
| Osborn warned the name might mislead | His 1924 text | — | Osborn 1924. **Unresolved:** the widely quoted wording ("may entirely mislead us as to its feeding habits and belie its character") should be checked against the Novitates scan before it is used verbatim anywhere on screen. The narration paraphrases and does not quote. |
| 1994: an egg of the same type contained an oviraptorid embryo | Embryo IGM 100/971, Ukhaa Tolgod, eggshell matching the 1923 clutch type | That the 1923 eggs were therefore oviraptorid | Norell et al. 1994, *Science* 266:779 |
| 1995: a close relative found crouched over a clutch, arms spread | IGM 100/979 in brooding posture on a nest | "Brooding" as behaviour | Norell et al. 1995, *Nature* 378:774; referred to *Citipati osmolskae* by Clark, Norell & Barsbold 2001 |
| The 1923 skeleton was itself a parent on its own nest | AMNH 6517 re-examined: brooding position over an oviraptorid nest; a tiny juvenile in the same nest | "Parent rather than predator" | Clark, Norell & Chiappe 1999; [Norell et al. 2018, *AMNH Novitates* 3899](https://bioone.org/journals/American-Museum-Novitates/volume-2018/issue-3899/3899.1/A-Second-Specimen-of-Citipati-Osmolskae-Associated-With-a-Nest/10.1206/3899.1.full) |
| "It still says egg thief" | The genus name is unchanged | — | Any current reference; [NHM Dino Directory](https://www.nhm.ac.uk/discover/dino-directory/oviraptor.html) |

**Boundaries kept.** *Citipati* is called "a close cousin", never merged with *Oviraptor*. The
nesting animals are "parent", never "mother" (medullary bone evidence exists for one specimen only;
the 2018 specimen had none). The narration says the eggs were its own; it does not say the animal
never ate eggs. The NHM still lists eggs and shellfish as possible foods, and diet remains debated.

**Unresolved.** (1) The exact Osborn wording, above. (2) "Seventy years later" is 1924 to 1994, the
embryo; the brooding specimen is 1995 and the holotype re-reading is 1999/2018. The line is
accurate for the embryo, which is the beat it follows, but an editor may prefer "decades later".
(3) The beat-3 Protoceratops visual shows it walking away from the eggs. That is a staging
choice to plant doubt, not a fossil observation; nothing in the record shows Protoceratops near
these eggs at all.

## 5. Weakest sentence, revised once

The first draft of beat 4 read: *"Then, in 1994, a team opened an identical egg. Inside was a
baby."* Two problems: "opened" implies an act that did not happen (the embryo was found in a
weathered egg and prepared out of the rock), and "identical" overstates what eggshell matching
supports. Revised and re-timed: *"Then, in 1994, the same kind of egg turned up with a baby
inside. Not the neighbour's. An oviraptor's."* (7.0 s, one word longer, both problems gone.)

If a second trim is wanted for word count, beat 3 loses "that nested nearby" with no loss of
meaning, since the visual already places the neighbour at the nest.

story's evidence is fossils in red sandstone and a period excavation, which a stylised vector or
low-poly look would turn into symbols. See the chat note on styles: a three-still frame-zero
