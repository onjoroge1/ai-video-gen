# Direction file template (World channel, illustrated long-form)

Write `topics/directions/<slug>.txt` from this template. The direction steers story and pictures; it is NOT a
source. Lessons from the bee films (2026-10-07/08):

- Assert NO fact, number, date or outcome that research has not cited. The writer copies direction text into
  narration and the claim ledger then refuses it ("stood among the honey giants" cost a run). Put specifics you
  want checked under "Verify", phrased as questions, never as statements.
- Opening: inside one person's practical problem, second person ("Imagine you're a ..."), the need tangible
  before any date or name; the decision with its rationale as sources give it; the ordinary act that released
  the consequence spoken ONCE before the hinge; the body never re-tells it.
- Close: return to the opening person's need or object, and only claim an outcome the sources support.
- Storyboard: one recurring story object drawn the same way every time; one explanatory cutaway on the
  mechanism (the relationship inside one picture, arrows allowed, no text); NO maps; people in frame, the
  viewpoint figure visually consistent; documented people look different and carry only documented actions.

## Template

<Channel line>: Bolt Explains the World: one sourced intervention backfire (an animal moved, an invention
adopted, or a rule enforced that did the opposite of its purpose), ~5-minute landscape
illustrated documentary.

OPENING: <who the viewer is, what they need, what stands in the way, in plain words>. Give the decision
(<the intervention>) its rationale as the sources give it. Reach the first unintended consequence as efficiently
as the story allows. The body begins after that consequence and never re-tells it.

Causal arc (as questions for research to answer, not facts): <what was done, by whom, when, why>?
<what happened to the target population>? <what happened next, in order>? <is there a documented reversal or
recovery, and what exactly does the source say>?

Verify: <specific numbers or claims to confirm or drop>. Separate documented fact from legend.

Research starting points: <2-4 reputable URLs: government, museum, university, peer-reviewed>.

Storyboard: <the one recurring object>; <the mechanism cutaway idea>; no maps; people in frame.

## Angle: unfolding (schedule item `"angle": "unfolding"`)

For a story that is still happening (cane toads and the ants, quolls and rodents now being tried
against them; carp and the barriers and harvest programmes). The topic winners in these lanes
are present-tense: the fight-back, not only the original mistake (measured 2026-10-11: "Australia
Used Ants to Destroy Millions of Cane Toads", 1.1M views on a 107k channel).

OPENING: inside one person's present-day practical problem with the consequence (a ranger, a
farmer, a fisher), second person, the need tangible before any date or name. The original
intervention is the backstory, told in one or two beats as the cause of today's problem.

Causal arc (as questions, not facts): what is the consequence costing people or wildlife today,
according to current sources? What countermeasures are being tried now, by whom, since when?
What do the measurements so far actually show, including what has NOT worked? What is still
unknown? Close on what the evidence supports today, never on a promise the sources do not make.

Verify: dates and results of every countermeasure named, and whether each result is a field
measurement, a trial, or a proposal. Prefer the last three years of government, university and
peer-reviewed sources; say plainly when a result is preliminary.
