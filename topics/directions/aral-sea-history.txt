Bolt Explains History: one sourced history story, ~5-minute landscape illustrated documentary.
The story shape is a state programme and what it cost (engine state_programme): a government plan
that worked on its own terms while a sea, and the people who lived from it, paid for it.

OPENING: put the viewer inside one ordinary fishing family's practical life in a port town on the
Aral Sea: the boats, the catch, the cannery, the work. Then the state's decision (to turn the
region's rivers onto the desert to grow cotton) with its reasoning as the sources give it, before
any official name. Reach the first consequence (the sea beginning to fall) within about forty
seconds of narration. The body begins after that consequence and never re-tells it.

Causal arc (as questions for research to answer, not facts): Why did Soviet planners want cotton
from Central Asia, and from when? Which rivers were diverted, how, and from when? Did the cotton
harvests rise, and by how much, as the sources state it? What happened to the water reaching the
sea, and then to its level, area and saltiness, year by year? What happened to the fishing
industry and the port towns? What did the exposed seabed do, and what health effects do the
sources document (state each exactly as sourced; never more)? Did anyone in authority know or
warn what the diversions would do to the sea, and what is recorded of it? What is the sea now: the
northern part and the dam built to save it, and the southern part?

STORY SPINE (which research fact fills which role; say each only as the sources state it):
- setup: the need the state set out to meet, as it stood before the programme.
- intervention (programme_imposed): the diversion of the rivers for irrigation, by whom, from when.
- false resolution (apparent_success, if sourced): the cotton the programme delivered.
- mechanism (harm_pathway): the diverted rivers no longer bringing the sea its water.
- escalation (harm_accumulates, several): the shrinking sea, the fisheries and port towns, the
  contaminated dust, each a distinct sourced step in the order it happened.
- reversal (cost_reckoned): what the sea and its people became, as record (for example the dried
  eastern basin, and the northern sea held behind its dam).
- verdict: the narrator's own conclusion restating the corrected claim; it cites no claim and names
  no event, person, number or object from the story, e.g. "It was never a natural disaster. It
  was a plan that worked."

Verify: the decade the diversions began (sources say the 1950s and 1960s); every figure for the
sea's area, volume, level or saltiness, with its year; any fish-catch or job figure, with its year;
the names of the port towns; the dam's name, completion year and effect on the northern sea; the
year the eastern basin dried; any statement that officials expected or accepted the sea's loss,
and exactly who said it and where it is recorded. Separate documented fact from later legend.

Research starting points (each checked 2026-10-14: it opens to an automated fetch):
- NASA Earth Observatory, "World of Change: Shrinking Aral Sea"
  (https://earthobservatory.nasa.gov/world-of-change/AralSea): the 1960s diversion project, the
  rivers turned onto farms for cotton, fisheries and the communities that depended on them
  collapsing, the contaminated dust from the exposed lakebed, the Kok-Aral dike and dam (2005).
- NASA Earth Observatory, "The Aral Sea Loses Its Eastern Lobe"
  (https://earthobservatory.nasa.gov/images/84437/the-aral-sea-loses-its-eastern-lobe): the Soviet
  government diverting the Amu Darya and Syr Darya to irrigate farmland in the 1950s and 1960s, and
  the eastern basin drying in 2014.
Prefer these over pages that refuse automated fetches; the mechanism and the reckoning must be
quoted from a page that opens.

Storyboard: the one recurring story object is a fishing boat on the shore of the port town, drawn
the same way every time, first afloat and later stranded on sand; the mechanism cutaway shows, in
one picture with arrows and no text, the two rivers turned into irrigation canals and cotton fields
while the sea receives nothing; no maps; people in frame; the viewpoint fishing family visually
consistent; documented people look different from one another and carry only documented actions.
No gore; any health harm is shown through people and their surroundings, never through illness
close-ups.
