Bolt Explains History: one sourced history story, ~5-minute landscape illustrated documentary.
The story shape is a verdict a whole community reached and later had to take back.

OPENING: put the viewer inside one ordinary villager's practical problem in Salem Village: a
neighbour's household is in distress, the minister and the magistrates are looking for a cause,
and the villager has a farm, a family and a reputation to keep. Give the community's decision
(to treat the afflicted girls' accusations as evidence of witchcraft) its reasoning as the sources
give it. Reach the first unintended consequence as efficiently as the story allows: the
accusations spreading beyond the first few people. The body begins after that consequence and
never re-tells it.

Causal arc (as questions for research to answer, not facts): What did the afflicted girls do, and
how did local leaders interpret it? Who were the first people accused, and why were they easy to
accuse? What was "spectral evidence" and why could an accused person not disprove it? How did
confessing versus denying change what happened to the accused? How did the accusations spread, and
to whom? What did the court do, and who stopped it, how, and when? What did the colony and its
people later say or do to acknowledge the wrong (apologies, reversals of attainder, compensation),
and what exactly do the sources say about each?

Verify: the number of people accused, jailed, executed and who died in custody; the dates of the
first accusations and of the last executions; the name and role of the special court; the role of
Governor Phips and of Increase Mather's argument about spectral evidence; Samuel Sewall's public
apology and its date; Ann Putnam Jr.'s apology and its date; the later legislative exonerations,
including the most recent one. Separate documented fact from later legend: how were the convicted
executed, and is any popular image of the trials unsupported by the records? Is any popular
modern explanation (for example ergot poisoning) a documented finding or a hypothesis?

Research starting points: the Salem Witch Trials Documentary Archive and Transcription Project,
University of Virginia (https://salem.lib.virginia.edu/); the Massachusetts General Court /
Commonwealth of Massachusetts records of the exoneration acts (https://malegislature.gov/);
the National Park Service, Salem Maritime National Historical Park pages on 1692
(https://www.nps.gov/sama/).

Storyboard: the one recurring story object is a handwritten deposition page (a court examination
record), drawn the same way every time; the mechanism cutaway shows spectral evidence as one
picture: an accuser pointing at an empty space where only she claims to see the accused's "shape",
while the real accused stands elsewhere, with arrows showing why denial cannot answer it, no text;
no maps; people in frame; the viewpoint villager visually consistent; documented people (the
magistrates, the minister, the governor) look different from one another and carry only documented
actions. No gore, no depiction of the executions themselves beyond the gallows hill at a distance.
