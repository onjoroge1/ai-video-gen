Bolt Explains the World: one sourced animal-intervention backfire, ~5-minute landscape illustrated documentary.
MYTH-BUSTER OPENING (run with --opening myth).

OPENING: the hook is the viewer's own guess, posed as a guess and never as a claim about what people believed: for
example "You'd think four hippos left behind on a ranch would simply die out." Use a myth line ONLY if a ledger
claim proves it wrong. Then: the bust (one cited sentence: the hippos multiplied -- the census figure as the source
states it -- NOT yet why), the danger (one cited sentence: declared invasive, a threat to the river ecosystem, as
sources state it), and the open question (for example "So why can't Colombia stop them?"). Then the viewer's role:
someone who lives or works along the river near the old estate, the need tangible (the river, the fishing, the
cattle) before any name. Give the decision (the hippos brought to a private zoo, and what happened to them after
their owner's death) as the sources give it. Reach the first unintended consequence (the hippos leaving the estate
for the rivers) within about forty seconds of narration.

Causal arc (as questions for research to answer, not facts): How many hippos were brought to Hacienda Napoles, of
which sexes, and when? What happened to them after Escobar's death in 1993? Where did they go, and how did the
population grow? What in Colombia lets them breed so fast (predators, droughts, climate, water)? What harm do they
do or threaten to rivers, native species and people, according to the sources? What has Colombia decided to do
(sterilization, relocation, culling), and what do the sources say about whether it is working?

MECHANISM (the answer to the opening question): the documented reason the population grows unchecked, stated as
the answer -- for example "Nothing here does what lions and droughts do in Africa" -- only as research supports it.

Verify: the founding count and sexes; the import year (sources differ: the 1980s, 1991); the 1993 date; every
population figure and its year and method; any projection (state it as a projection); the government's plan and
its date. Separate documented fact from legend; do not dramatize attacks beyond what a source states.
