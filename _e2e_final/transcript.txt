Don't scratch — too late, right? Every itch is a tiny alarm your skin sets off. Why? Special skin cells called mast cells respond by dumping a chemical: histamine. That histamine docks onto itch nerve endings, firing a signal straight up your spinal cord. Your brain's somatosensory cortex reads the alarm and demands one thing: scratch. Scratching fires faster pain-touch nerves that drown out the itch signal — instant relief. Here's the trick: scratching works by replacing the itch with mild pain your brain prefers. But that scratching damages skin, making mast cells dump MORE histamine — itch gets worse. So don't scratch — but it's already too late, right?
